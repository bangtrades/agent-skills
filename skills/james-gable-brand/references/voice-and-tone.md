# Voice and Tone

## Voice principles

### 1. Specialist, not self-important

Name the healthcare context and the decision in front of the client. Specificity creates authority; superlatives do not.

- Prefer: “We evaluate loss experience, coverage structure, retention, and market options before recommending a renewal path.”
- Avoid: “We deliver unparalleled, best-in-class insurance solutions.”

### 2. Objective and consultative

The observed site repeatedly frames the work as an “objective, independent evaluation.” Show the criteria, tradeoffs, and sequence of work.

- Prefer: “A higher retention may improve economics, but it also changes volatility and capital requirements. We model the tradeoff with your team.”
- Avoid: “A captive is the smarter choice.”

### 3. Results-aware, never absolute

Connect the work to claims, cost of risk, resilience, and decision quality, but qualify outcomes.

- Prefer: “Designed to identify preventable loss drivers and improve the quality of renewal decisions.”
- Avoid: “Proven to cut claims and premiums.”

### 4. Relationship-led

James & Gable’s advantage is specialist continuity. Write as an advisor who will remain through analysis, placement, implementation, and year-round risk work.

- Prefer: “Your broker and risk advisor stay involved from program review through renewal and follow-through.”
- Avoid: “Submit your details to our platform.”

### 5. Calm under pressure

Insurance and clinical-risk communication should reduce ambiguity. Use short, declarative sentences and concrete next steps. Do not manufacture urgency or dramatize loss.

## Style rules

- Use plain English first; define insurance terms when the audience may not know them.
- Prefer active voice and named actors.
- Write “healthcare organization” unless a specific segment is known.
- Use “program,” “coverage,” “retention,” “risk financing,” and “claims experience” precisely.
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
- Keep exclamation marks out of institutional communications.
- Avoid “leverage,” “synergy,” “revolutionize,” “seamless,” “cutting-edge,” “AI-powered,” and “one-stop shop” unless the exact meaning is explained.
- Do not use fear, scarcity, countdowns, or “act now” patterns.
- Do not call the brand independent when discussing ownership; it operates within Trucordia.

## Signature language

Use selectively, not as mandatory boilerplate:

- “Insurance professionals serving the healthcare industry”
- “Tailored insurance solutions”
- “Objective, independent evaluation”
- “Reduce claims and long-term cost of risk” only as a qualified aim, not a guarantee
- “First class, full service” only when the service scope is immediately made concrete

## Tone by channel

### Website

Confident, concise, educational. Lead with the client decision and give a visible path to evidence and a licensed advisor.

### Proposal and renewal presentation

Analytical and board-ready. State current position, options, recommendation, financial/risk implications, source date, and decision required.

### Risk insight

Useful and measured. Separate observed trend, interpretation, and action. Cite sources and explain whether findings apply broadly or only to a segment.

### LinkedIn

Human and expert. One practical observation, one implication, one question or resource. Avoid hashtags unless distribution practice demonstrates value; use no more than two.

### Email

Direct and service-oriented. Put the decision or requested action in the first two sentences. Avoid promotional filler.

### Incident or claim-sensitive communication

Empathetic, factual, and private. Do not speculate about fault, coverage, clinical quality, or legal outcome. Route sensitive discussion to approved secure channels.

### AI-supported interaction

Transparent and bounded. Say what the tool can organize or explain, identify the licensed professional who reviews the output, and make clear that final coverage and placement decisions are not automated.
