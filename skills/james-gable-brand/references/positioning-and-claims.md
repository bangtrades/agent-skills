# Positioning and Claims

## Positioning statement

For healthcare organizations facing complex insurance, claims, and risk-financing decisions, James & Gable is the specialist insurance and risk-advisory team that connects program structure with clinical and employee risk reduction. Its relationship continuity is supported by Trucordia’s broader market access, operating infrastructure, data, and technology.

## Differentiation pillars

### Healthcare specialization

Make the sector visible in examples, coverage priorities, risk language, and the backgrounds of approved experts. Do not rely on the word “specialist” alone.

### Objective program evaluation

Show the assessment dimensions: loss history, coverage, limits, retention, premiums, operational risk, and financing alternatives. “Independent” describes advisory judgment, not corporate independence from Trucordia.

### Risk reduction connected to brokerage

The RN/JD, clinical safety, employee safety, and loss-trending capabilities are distinctive. Explain how they inform program decisions without promising that interventions will reduce claims.

### Specialist relationship, scaled platform

Use James & Gable for category relevance and continuity; use Trucordia for market access, systems, capital, and national scale. Do not obscure the parent or make stale standalone-size claims.

## Claim classes

### Class A — stable observed facts

May be used after normal editorial review:

- James & Gable serves the healthcare industry.
- The observed service set includes brokerage, program evaluation, risk-financing alternatives, and risk reduction.
- The current logo pairs James & Gable with Trucordia.

### Class B — dated, attributable facts

Include source and date; revalidate before major release:

- Trucordia’s top-20 position, 5,000+ team members, 200+ offices, and $5.7 billion valuation.
- The 2021 James & Gable / Chivaroli combination and its claimed scale.
- Vertafore deployment and Trucordia Go capabilities.
- Public hospital program details and broker participation.

### Class C — outcomes and comparative claims

Require written source, scope, method, client permission where applicable, legal/compliance approval, and an expiration date:

- claim-frequency or severity reduction;
- premium savings;
- total-cost-of-risk improvement;
- “proven,” “largest,” “leading,” “best,” or comparative performance;
- specific client program results.

### Class D — unresolved claims

Do not normalize until the source of truth is resolved:

- “Founded in 1991.” The site says it has served healthcare since 1991; 2021 press boilerplate says founded in 1993.
- Current headquarters or office network.
- Exact J&G headcount, revenue, client count, or national rank.
- Current legal status of “Chivaroli, James & Gable.”

## Required language controls

- Say **“designed to identify claim drivers”**, not “will reduce claims.”
- Say **“can help improve the long-term cost of risk”**, not “guarantees lower cost.”
- Say **“subject to underwriting, carrier approval, and market terms”** where coverage or placement may be inferred.
- Say **“serving the healthcare industry since 1991”** only as the brand’s observed service-history claim and only if currently approved. Do not translate it into a legal founding year.
- Attribute **“one of the largest premier full-service healthcare brokers”** to the August 2021 combined-platform announcement; do not use it as an evergreen J&G-alone claim.
- Keep the Sonoma Valley Hospital BETA rate comparison client-specific; never turn 15–20% into a general savings claim.

## Competitive posture

Against global firms such as Marsh and Aon, emphasize specialist continuity and practical execution, not superior scale. Against Gallagher, HUB, Alliant, and EPIC, emphasize the integration of clinical/employee risk reduction with program structure and the support of Trucordia. Any direct comparison requires current, like-for-like evidence.

## Client evidence protocol

Before publishing a client name, logo, quotation, program detail, or result:

1. confirm the information is not confidential merely because it is public;
2. obtain written client and internal approval;
3. state the period and program scope;
4. identify external factors and limitations;
5. avoid PHI and individual claim detail;
6. set a review or expiration date.

## AI and digital-service guardrails

- AI may organize intake, summarize approved documents, explain defined concepts, prepare questions, or support advisors.
- AI must not bind coverage, determine eligibility, replace licensed advice, or deliver unreviewed recommendations presented as final.
- Clearly identify when a licensed professional reviews or takes over.
- Do not put PHI, claim files, credentials, or confidential client information into unapproved models or channels.
- “AI-enabled” is not a benefit by itself. Name the user task improved and the human/accountability boundary.

## Privacy and regulatory posture

Use the current Trucordia privacy framework for applicable data collection. Collect only what is needed, explain why, route sensitive material to approved secure systems, and avoid presenting marketing content as legal, clinical, actuarial, or coverage advice.
