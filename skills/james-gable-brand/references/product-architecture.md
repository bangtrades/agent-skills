# Product Architecture

## Organizing principle

Organize the offer around the healthcare client’s decision lifecycle, then expose coverage and technical depth within it. Do not make a flat list of policies the main navigation.

## Client journey

### 1. Understand the current position

- program due diligence;
- loss-history and claim-trend review;
- coverage, limit, retention, and premium analysis;
- operational, clinical, and employee-safety risk review.

**Output:** baseline findings, data gaps, and decision priorities.

### 2. Design the risk strategy

- conventional risk transfer;
- large deductibles and self-insured retentions;
- self-insurance;
- captives and other risk-funding alternatives;
- limit, tower, and coverage-structure options.

**Output:** option set with financial, volatility, governance, and implementation tradeoffs.

### 3. Access and place the market

- carrier strategy and submissions;
- terms and coverage comparison;
- negotiation and placement;
- binding and documentation through licensed professionals.

**Output:** approved placement recommendation and implementation plan.

### 4. Reduce and manage risk

- clinical and employee safety consulting;
- loss trending and targeted interventions;
- risk evaluations and assessments;
- policy/program development;
- regulatory education and training;
- RN/JD advisory access;
- claims and renewal follow-through.

**Output:** year-round action plan and governance cadence.

## Audience pathways

### Health systems and hospitals

Lead with complex multi-line coordination, professional/general liability, property and catastrophe, cyber, D&O/EPL, workers’ compensation, claims, and board-ready renewal governance.

### Medical groups and clinics

Lead with medical malpractice, general liability, cyber/privacy, employment, property, regulatory/billing E&O, and operational continuity.

### Senior living and long-term care

Lead with professional/general liability severity, workforce, property, emergency planning, safety programs, and retention strategy.

### Managed care and health plans

Lead with managed-care E&O, stop loss, cyber, D&O/EPL, fiduciary, regulatory exposure, and enterprise risk.

### Ancillary healthcare providers

Lead with role-specific professional liability, general liability, property, auto, cyber, crime, and contractual requirements.

### Universities and municipal healthcare entities

Use only when the combined-platform capability is current and approved. Lead with public governance, broad risk portfolio, procurement, documentation, and stakeholder communication.

Coverage lists are educational. Availability, terms, limits, and suitability are client- and market-specific.

## Risk-maturity model

1. **Transfer:** primarily insured with conventional deductibles.
2. **Retain:** larger deductibles or self-insured retentions with stronger claims governance.
3. **Finance:** self-insurance, captive, or structured alternatives supported by actuarial and capital analysis.
4. **Integrate:** insurance, claims, clinical/employee safety, and executive governance operate as one cost-of-risk program.

Never present maturity as a score of organizational quality. It describes financing and governance complexity, not superiority.
